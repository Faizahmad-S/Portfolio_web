Purpose

How good are London's food businesses, and where are the poor ones? I took the Food Standards Agency's Food Hygiene Rating data for every food business in London and worked through it from the API to a findings deck.

I also wanted a repeatable way to explore a new dataset: a fixed run of questions that goes from "can I trust this data?" to "what should someone do with it?", with one SQL file per step.

Tools

Python | pandas | SQLAlchemy | PostgreSQL | Window Functions | CTEs | Jupyter | VS Code

Summary

I pulled 79,727 records for 33 London local authorities from the FSA API, flattened and cleaned them in pandas, and loaded them into PostgreSQL. The analysis runs across 15 SQL scripts: structure, a data quality audit, distributions, segment benchmarks, change over time, rating freshness and relationships between the inspection scores.

The audit changed the whole analysis. 9,887 businesses (12.4%) have no real rating and carry a fake 1901 date, so I built one clean view, fsa_rated, that every later query reads from.

What I Did

  • Stopped every percentage being understated (five-star would have read 59.9% instead of 68.3%) by auditing the data first and finding that the 9,887 unrated businesses and the 1901 placeholder dates were the same rows, then filtering them out in a single SQL view.
  • Found a 39-point gap in five-star share between the best and worst boroughs (City of London 87.8%, Newham 49.1%) by benchmarking every borough against the London average with RANK(), ignoring groups under 30 businesses.
  • Showed the gap isn't just down to what businesses sell by using a CTE to work out what each borough should score for its mix of business types. Newham should average 4.43 and actually averages 3.94.
  • Traced where the poor ratings sit: 8 of 33 boroughs hold 53% of them, and "Retailers - other" make up 19.1% of businesses but 30.8% of 0-2 ratings, using running totals and FILTER.
  • Pinned down which inspection score separates failing businesses from excellent ones (confidence in management, a 12.2-point gap against 8.7 for hygiene) with conditional averages and CORR().

Key Insights

  • Most businesses are doing well: 68.3% are rated 5 and 86.0% are rated 4 or 5.
  • The risk is in a thin tail. 5.5% are rated 0 to 2, which is still 3,857 premises, and 283 of them are rated 0.
  • Newham, Waltham Forest and Ealing sit well below what their business mix predicts, while Kensington and Chelsea, the City of London and Merton beat it.
  • Recent inspections look worse (6 to 9% rated 0-2 against about 1% in 2022-23), but that's most likely a selection effect: poor performers get re-inspected sooner. I've reported it as a limit, not a decline.
  • Ratings are fairly current: 45.5% were awarded in the last 12 months and only 3.7% are over five years old.

Outcome

A reproducible workflow from API to insight: two notebooks, 15 ordered SQL scripts, one clean analysis view and a 10-slide findings deck. Next I'd like to join deprivation data to test why some boroughs score lower, and build a Power BI dashboard on fsa_rated.
