01 — About

I've seen the numbers from both sides

At Greggs I run shifts where stock, takings, waste and food safety are my responsibility. I check live sales and waste in Power BI during the day, and I've cut wastage by 2.7% with daily stock checks. So when I analyse a dataset, I'm used to asking what the numbers mean for the people who have to act on them.

I finished my MSc in Data Analytics at Oxford Brookes in 2024, with a dissertation on whether financial news sentiment tracks share prices. Since then I've built projects in PostgreSQL, Python and Power BI, and in October 2026 I became a DataCamp certified Associate Data Analyst.

My latest project, London Food Retail Insights, looks at hygiene ratings for all 79,726 food businesses in London. The most useful thing it taught me was to check the data first: 1 in 8 records wasn't a real rating, and leaving them in would have made every percentage wrong.

02 — How I Work

The same steps on every project

step_1

Check

Can I trust it? Duplicates, blanks, placeholder values and wrong types get found and fixed before anything else.

step_2

Explore

Look at each column on its own, then compare groups against a benchmark, and only trust groups big enough to matter.

step_3

Test

Check the obvious explanation before believing a result, and say so when the data can't answer the question.

step_4

Report

A dashboard, a deck or a short write-up that someone can act on without reading the SQL behind it.
